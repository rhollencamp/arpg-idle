# Vision & Pillars

## Elevator pitch

A fog has swallowed the world, and things live inside it. Your town is gathered
around **the last lighthouse** still burning. The light keeps the fog back, and
survivors wandering in the murk can see it and find their way in.

You are the **Keeper**. You never fight. You decide who goes out, what they
carry, and how they behave out there. Then you watch the light and wait.

## Tone

- Grim, but with warmth. Your people are ordinary: a fisher, a cooper, a
  disgraced priest. They are brave because they have no other choice.
- Understated, slightly dry narration in the expedition logs. The humor comes
  from how things play out, not from jokes.
- The fog is a mystery, not a demon army. The enemy is closer to weather with
  teeth than to an evil overlord. Why the fog exists is a question for later.

## Pillars

1. **The quality of your plans decides the outcome, not how often you click.**
   What you decide in the active phase (loadout, team, policies, town
   priorities) should decide what happens in the idle phase. Checking in more
   often should never matter more than planning well.

2. **Every person is both a soldier and a worker.** Characters come from one
   shared pool. Someone out on an expedition isn't working the oil press or
   standing on the wall. Deciding where each person goes is the central
   decision in the game.

3. **Light is the resource that matters.** Lantern oil sets how deep an
   expedition can go. The lighthouse's strength sets how far the fog stays
   back and how many refugees find you. Progress should *feel* like pushing
   back the dark.

4. **Losses are real, and they tell a story.** Permadeath applies to
   characters and to the town itself. A fallen town is a hard reset. Losses
   should make sense after the fact
   ("I sent them out with half a flask of oil") and should never feel random
   or unfair.

5. **The log is the show.** The game has no real-time combat graphics. The
   drama lives in the expedition report. It should be worth reading.

6. **Truly idle.** The world keeps running while the game is closed.
   Expeditions finish, the town produces, and the siege keeps pressing.
   Returning to the game is reading what happened, not collecting a timer.

## Inspirations

- **Diablo:** loot excitement, character builds, item affixes.
- **Darkest Dungeon:** a roster of fragile people, injuries and quirks,
  a town between runs.
- **FF12 Gambits:** priority rules that make policies deep.
- **Frostpunk:** a town under existential pressure, where every person counts.
- **Idle/incremental games:** offline progress, and longer cycles unlocked
  over time.
